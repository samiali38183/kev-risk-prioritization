Sample design: all 1779 KEV CVEs plus 5000 non-KEV CVEs drawn with a fixed seed from the 403085 non-KEV CVEs in the EPSS file (404864 CVEs scored in total; KEV is 0.44% of them). Population-level counts for non-KEV CVEs come from the full file at fetch time and are stored in `data/snapshot/SNAPSHOT.json`.

## Caveats that apply to every number below

- **Circularity.** EPSS is trained on observed exploitation evidence, and its published list of data partners includes a known-exploited-vulnerabilities feed (VulnCheck KEV), a separate catalog whose overlap with the CISA catalog was not measured here. EPSS separating KEV from non-KEV is therefore partly the model recovering its own training signal, not an independent validation. These numbers describe the relationship between two related signals; they do not show that either one improves remediation outcomes.
- **KEV is an incomplete label.** A non-KEV CVE is not known to be unexploited; it is only absent from the catalog. Exploited-but-unlisted CVEs sit in the non-KEV group, which depresses every precision figure.
- **A single snapshot, no time dimension.** Scores were read after most KEV entries were added, so EPSS may already reflect the listing. This is not a forecast test.

## 2. Thresholds: recall and precision

Recall is exact (every KEV CVE is in the snapshot). The non-KEV count is the exact count over the whole EPSS file, so precision below is the population value (KEV prevalence 0.44%), not the sample's inflated one. Sample columns show the same rule applied to the 5000 sampled non-KEV CVEs.

| EPSS >= | KEV flagged | Recall | Non-KEV flagged (all 403085) | Precision (population) | Non-KEV flagged in sample (n=5000) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 1338 | 75.2% | 16203 (4.02%) | 7.6% | 192 (3.84%) |
| 0.5 | 909 | 51.1% | 3473 (0.86%) | 20.7% | 41 (0.82%) |
| 0.9 | 489 | 27.5% | 337 (0.08%) | 59.2% | 4 (0.08%) |

Read this as: a threshold of 0.1 reaches most KEV CVEs but flags many times more non-KEV CVEs than KEV ones at population prevalence, so most flagged CVEs are not in the catalog. Whether those are false alarms or unlisted exploitation cannot be told from this data.

## 3. The tool on a mixed list (KEV + sampled non-KEV, invented assets)

`data/snapshot/mixed_findings.csv`: 2000 findings = 100 KEV + 1900 non-KEV CVEs from the snapshot, with invented asset, ACR 1-8 and days open, no CVSS. Run through `kev_prioritize.py` with its default weights (CVSS term dropped and the rest renormalized).

| Ranking | KEV in top 50 | KEV in top 100 | KEV in top 200 | Rank of last KEV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk score (tool) | 50 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| EPSS only | 39 | 65 | 82 | 1440 |
| ACR only (control) | 4 | 10 | 16 | 1930 |

With 100 KEV rows in the list, a perfect top-100 would contain 100. The tool's top 100 holds 100 KEV and 0 non-KEV findings; 0 KEV findings rank below position 100.

This result is largely **by construction**: the KEV term carries 0.40 of the weight, so KEV matches sort ahead of most non-KEV findings unless ACR and EPSS differ widely (a non-KEV finding on ACR 8 with EPSS near 1 can still outscore a KEV finding on ACR 1 with EPSS near 0). It shows the formula behaves as described on realistic EPSS values; it is not evidence that the ranking reduces risk.
